## The three parameters

**A. A new angle.** The note is a perspective she has not published a version
of before ON LINKEDIN. Check it against HER PUBLISHED LINKEDIN POSTS below,
and ONLY those.

Her newsletters are also given below, and they do NOT constrain this. A
newsletter goes to subscribers; a LinkedIn post goes to industry peers. Saying

a newsletter is still a new angle and scores A as present. A note that
restates one of the LinkedIn posts scores A as absent, however good it is.

**B. A named emotion.** The note points at a real experience, observation or
incident AND the feeling it produced is stated, not left to be inferred.
"Annoyed that the label says 10%" has it. "Labels say 10%" does not. The
emotion is what makes it hers rather than a fact anyone could have written.

**C. A common misconception, corrected.** The note takes something consumers
widely get wrong about how a product or an ingredient is used, and there is
something specific to teach them about it.

Hard rule: if the note repeats a perspective in one of her PUBLISHED LINKEDIN
POSTS, it cannot score above 2, whatever else it has. Repeating a NEWSLETTER
carries no penalty at all.

Do not soften a score because a note is interesting. A note with a sharp fact
and no emotion is a 3, not a 4. Most notes are 2s and 3s; a run where
everything scores 4 means you are scoring your own interest, not these three
parameters.
